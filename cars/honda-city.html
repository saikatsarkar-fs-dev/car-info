Honda City

The sedan that refused to leave the conversation. The City still does the things sedans do best: a low, comfortable ride, a spacious rear seat and an engine that loves to rev. The hybrid adds real efficiency without asking for a charging cable.

01 The short version

The City has been the default premium sedan for so long that it is easy to forget how good the basics are. The rear seat is wide and supportive, the ride is settled at highway speed, and the 1.5-litre petrol engine is one of the smoothest in the market. It is not a sports sedan, but it is a lovely thing to drive at seven-tenths.

The e:HEV is the interesting one. A 1.5-litre Atkinson-cycle petrol engine works with an electric motor to produce 126 PS and 253 Nm, and the result is a calm, quick-enough city car that returns a claimed 27.26 km/l. The battery eats into the boot, though: 306 litres instead of 506.

The City's weakness is the market itself. Buyers have moved to SUVs, and the City's 165 mm clearance is modest for Indian speed breakers. If you want a sedan, it remains one of the best; if you want a do-everything family car, the Creta and Nexon make a stronger case.

04 Safety and equipment

Six airbags, vehicle stability assist and tyre-pressure monitoring are standard. The top variants add Honda Sensing, a suite of driver-assistance features that includes adaptive cruise control, lane-keeping assist and collision mitigation. LaneWatch, a camera that shows the blind spot on the left, is also part of the City's toolkit.

05 The ownership notes

What works

  • Rear-seat comfort and ride quality are still class-leading.
  • The petrol engine is smooth and happy to rev.
  • The hybrid returns a claimed 27.26 km/l.
  • The petrol's 506-litre boot is huge for a sedan.

What to weigh up

  • 165 mm of clearance is modest for Indian roads.
  • The hybrid's boot shrinks to 306 litres.
  • The petrol CVT is relaxed rather than quick.
  • Top hybrid variants are expensive.

Stretch for the hybrid if you can. In traffic it runs on electric power more often, and the efficiency gap over the petrol is large. The petrol CVT is still a lovely engine, just not a fast one. Drive both before you decide.
