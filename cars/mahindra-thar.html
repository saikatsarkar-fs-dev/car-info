Mahindra Thar

The most obviously off-road-focused car here. The Thar OG is still a short-wheelbase, body-on-frame, four-seat machine with a real low-range transfer case, but the 2026 car is quieter, more comfortable and finally has the safety kit to match its price.

01 The short version

The Thar has always been a compromise: brilliant off-road, crude on it. The OG version narrows that gap. The new chassis and retuned suspension make it more settled on broken tarmac, the cabin is quieter, and six airbags are now standard. It is still a three-door, four-seat short-wheelbase SUV, so don't expect Creta levels of space or refinement.

The engine range starts with a 1.5-litre diesel driving the rear wheels, moves up to a 2.2-litre diesel with optional four-wheel drive, and tops out with a 2.0-litre turbo petrol. The 4x4 models get a low-range transfer case and enough clearance to make a forest track look like a driveway.

If you actually use the capability, the Thar is hard to beat for the money. If you don't, the ride, the steering and the fuel bill will remind you every day that you bought a truck.

04 Safety and equipment

Six airbags, electronic stability control with roll-over mitigation, all-wheel disc brakes and tyre-pressure monitoring are part of the standard safety package. The 4x4 variants add hill-descent control, and front and rear parking sensors help in tight spaces. The Thar is safer than the car it replaces, but it is still a tall, short-wheelbase 4x4 with the physics that come with that shape.

05 The ownership notes

What works

  • Real off-road hardware: low range, 232 mm clearance and 650 mm wading.
  • The 2.2 diesel has the torque for loaded climbs.
  • Six airbags and a stronger safety package than before.
  • Nothing else at the price looks remotely like it.

What to weigh up

  • Only four seats and tight rear-seat access.
  • The short wheelbase still jitters on broken roads.
  • Heavy steering and a large turning circle in town.
  • The petrol is thirsty, and the boot is tiny.

Pick the 4x4 only if you need it. The rear-wheel-drive 1.5 diesel is the city-friendly Thar. The 2.2 diesel 4x4 is the one for the mountains. If you never leave the tarmac, the 4x4 costs you weight, money and fuel for capability you will not use.
