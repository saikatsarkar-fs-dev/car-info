Maruti Suzuki Swift

The default city car for a generation. The current Swift trades a cylinder for a lighter body, a sharper face and the kind of efficiency that makes a long Sunday drive feel almost free.

01 The short version

The Swift is the car Maruti builds when it wants to remind everyone who the volume champion is. The fourth-generation car is longer and wider than the one it replaced, but it still weighs very little, which is why 82 PS feels adequate rather than overwhelmed. The 1.2-litre three-cylinder engine is tuned for low-speed drivability and fuel economy. It is not a hot hatch, and it does not pretend to be.

Inside, the cabin is simple and durable. Top variants get a 9-inch touchscreen and automatic climate control; lower trims are much more basic. Rear-seat space is fine for two adults, tight for three, and the 265-litre boot is enough for a weekend, not a month.

The Swift makes most sense as a city car that occasionally does highway duty. The AMT is the easy choice in traffic; the manual is lighter and more engaging. Either way, the running costs are the headline.

04 Safety and equipment

Six airbags are standard, along with ABS with EBD, electronic stability programme and ISOFIX child-seat anchors. Top variants add a reverse camera, a 9-inch touchscreen and automatic climate control. The Swift's safety story is adequate for its price, but it is not the class leader.

05 The ownership notes

What works

  • Very low running costs and a wide service network.
  • Light controls and a tight turning circle make city parking easy.
  • Six airbags standard across the range.
  • The AMT is smooth enough for daily traffic.

What to weigh up

  • The three-cylinder engine sounds busy under load.
  • 163 mm of clearance is only just enough on bad speed breakers.
  • The rear seat is best for two adults.
  • The 265-litre boot trails newer compact SUVs.

CNG note. The Swift CNG is worth a look if you do high mileage and have a filling station nearby. It gives up some power and boot space, but the running cost is hard to argue with. Check the local price difference before choosing it over the petrol AMT.
