Toyota Fortuner

The full-size benchmark. Body-on-frame, seven seats, and a 2.8-litre diesel that makes light work of a loaded highway run. The Fortuner is expensive, thirsty and unapologetically old-school, which is exactly why people keep buying it.

01 The short version

The Fortuner is a ladder-frame SUV in an age of car-based crossovers, and that is the point. It is built to carry seven people, a full boot and a bad road without complaint. The 2.8-litre diesel makes 204 PS and up to 500 Nm in automatic form, which is enough to make a loaded overtake feel casual.

The trade-off is everything that comes with the architecture: weight, thirst and a cabin that feels more utilitarian than a modern monocoque SUV. The third row is usable for children and short adults, not a long-distance lounge. The 80-litre tank gives it a long highway range, which is exactly what the car is for.

In 2026 the range includes the Leader Edition, but the fundamentals are unchanged. If you need a tow car, a family expedition vehicle or a car that will still feel solid after a decade of bad roads, the Fortuner makes a strong case. If you mostly drive in the city, it is a lot of car to park.

04 Safety and equipment

Seven airbags, vehicle stability control, hill-start assist and tyre-pressure monitoring are part of the package. The 4x4 variants add a rear differential lock, and top trims get a 360-degree camera. The Fortuner's safety kit is comprehensive, but the cabin technology is a step behind the best car-based SUVs at this price.

05 The ownership notes

What works

  • The 2.8 diesel's 500 Nm makes loaded driving effortless.
  • Seven seats, an 80-litre tank and real highway range.
  • Body-on-frame toughness and strong resale value.
  • 4x4 with low range and a rear differential lock.

What to weigh up

  • It is enormous in city traffic and parking lots.
  • Fuel economy is not its strong suit.
  • The third row is best for children or short trips.
  • Top variants are luxury-SUV money.

The 4x2 diesel automatic is the sweet spot. Most buyers will never use low range, and the rear-drive car is lighter, cheaper and just as comfortable. The 2.7 petrol is smooth but thirsty; the 2.8 diesel is the reason most people buy this car.
