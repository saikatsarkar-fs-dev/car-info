02 / The route

Why these six?

Most Indian car conversations start with a budget and a use case, not a badge. These six cover the spread.

The Swift is the baseline city car. The Creta, Nexon and City carry the middle of the market. The Thar and Fortuner are the outliers, built for the roads that end where the map does.

Each car page gives you the numbers that matter, a short verdict, and the sources behind the figures. No star ratings, no sponsored rankings, no JavaScript.

  1. Stop 01

    The everyday

    The Swift is light, efficient and easy to thread through a city that was not designed for cars. It is the car most people actually need, even if it is not the one they want.

  2. Stop 02

    The family

    Creta, Nexon and City cover the middle of the market with petrol, diesel and hybrid choices. Five seats, a real boot and enough ground clearance for a broken service road.

  3. Stop 03

    The long road

    The Thar and Fortuner are body-on-frame, high-clearance machines. One is a short-wheelbase off-roader; the other is a seven-seat highway ship. Both make a detour look tempting.

A note on the numbers. Prices are ex-showroom, October 2026, and vary by city and variant. Specifications are checked against manufacturer pages; each car page lists its sources. Photos are hotlinked from Wikimedia Commons and credited in every caption and on the credits page.
